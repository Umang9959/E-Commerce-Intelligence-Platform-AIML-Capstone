# E-Commerce Support Knowledge Playbook

Q: What information is needed to help with an order issue?
A: Ask for the order number, customer email or phone used at checkout, item name, and a short description of the issue. This demo cannot access live order systems, so it should explain what information a real support team would need.

Q: How should a customer check order status?
A: Customers should use the official order tracking page, account order history, shipping confirmation email, or support channel. This demo can explain the process but cannot retrieve live shipment data.

Q: What does order processing mean?
A: Processing usually means the order was received and is being prepared for packing, payment verification, stock allocation, or warehouse handoff. It does not always mean the package has shipped.

Q: What does shipped mean?
A: Shipped means the package was handed to a carrier or marked ready for carrier pickup. Tracking may take several hours to update after the label is created.

Q: Why might tracking not update immediately?
A: Tracking can lag when the carrier has not scanned the package yet, when scans are delayed, or when the label was created before physical pickup. Customers should check again later or contact official support if it remains unchanged.

Q: What should a customer do if tracking says delivered but the package is missing?
A: Ask the customer to check delivery areas, household members, building reception, neighbors, and carrier proof-of-delivery. If still missing, they should contact official support with the order and tracking number.

Q: What should a customer do if only part of the order arrived?
A: Some orders ship in multiple packages. The customer should check whether there are separate tracking numbers and contact support if an item is missing from all shipments.

Q: What should a customer do if the wrong item arrived?
A: The customer should keep the item, packaging, invoice, and photos, then contact official support with the order number and item details so a replacement or return can be arranged.

Q: What should a customer do if an item arrived damaged?
A: The customer should photograph the item, packaging, shipping label, and invoice, then contact official support promptly. The answer should not promise a refund, but can explain that proof helps resolution.

Q: What should a customer do if an order is delayed?
A: The customer should check tracking, carrier alerts, holidays, weather disruption, and the estimated delivery window. If the order is beyond the expected window, they should contact official support.

Q: Can a customer change the delivery address after ordering?
A: Address changes may be possible only before shipment or through the carrier after dispatch. This demo cannot change addresses and should direct customers to official support quickly.

Q: Can a customer change the phone number after ordering?
A: Phone number changes usually require official support or account settings before shipment. This demo can explain the route but cannot modify live order data.

Q: Can a customer combine two orders into one shipment?
A: Combining orders depends on warehouse timing and is often not available once orders are placed. The customer should contact official support before fulfillment begins.

Q: Can a customer split one order into multiple shipments?
A: Split shipment depends on inventory and fulfillment rules. Some stores split automatically if items are in different warehouses or have different availability.

Q: Why was an order automatically cancelled?
A: Automatic cancellations may happen because of payment failure, stock unavailability, fraud checks, duplicate orders, address validation problems, or missed verification.

Q: How can a customer cancel an order?
A: Customers should use the official cancellation option in their account or contact support. Cancellation is usually easier before packing or shipment.

Q: Can an order be cancelled after shipment?
A: Once shipped, cancellation is usually not possible. The customer may need to refuse delivery where supported or start a return after receiving the package.

Q: What if a customer ordered by mistake?
A: The customer should cancel quickly through the official account or support channel. If the order has already shipped, they may need to follow the return process.

Q: What if a customer entered the wrong quantity?
A: If fulfillment has not started, the customer may be able to cancel or modify the order through official support. If shipped, they may need to return extra items.

Q: What if a customer forgot to apply a coupon?
A: Some stores do not apply coupons retroactively. The customer should contact official support, but the demo should not promise price adjustment.

Q: How do returns usually work?
A: A customer usually starts a return request, confirms eligibility, packages the item, attaches the return label if provided, and waits for inspection before refund or exchange.

Q: What items are often not returnable?
A: Common non-returnable items include opened hygiene products, personalized items, perishables, final-sale items, gift cards, and products damaged by misuse. The official policy decides eligibility.

Q: How long does a customer usually have to return an item?
A: Return windows vary by store and category. The answer should tell customers to check the official return policy and order date rather than invent a fixed number.

Q: Does the item need original packaging for a return?
A: Many stores ask for original packaging, tags, manuals, accessories, and invoice. If packaging is missing, the customer should contact support to confirm options.

Q: Who pays for return shipping?
A: Return shipping depends on policy and reason. Defective, damaged, or wrong-item returns may be treated differently from change-of-mind returns.

Q: How should a customer pack a return?
A: The item should be protected, include all accessories, use secure packaging, and include the return authorization or invoice when required.

Q: What if the customer lost the return label?
A: The customer should reopen the return request or contact official support for a replacement label. This demo cannot issue labels.

Q: Can a customer return a gift?
A: Gift returns depend on store policy. Support may require order details, gift receipt, recipient information, or original purchaser approval.

Q: Can a customer exchange instead of refund?
A: Exchanges depend on stock, category, price changes, and policy. Customers should select exchange during the return flow if available or ask official support.

Q: Can a customer return one item from a bundle?
A: Bundle returns may require returning the full bundle or adjusting the refund based on bundle discount. The official policy controls the outcome.

Q: What happens after a returned item is received?
A: The store usually inspects the item, confirms eligibility, then processes refund, exchange, store credit, or rejection depending on condition and policy.

Q: Why was a return rejected?
A: A return can be rejected if it is outside the return window, missing parts, used beyond inspection, damaged by misuse, final sale, or not matching the return request.

Q: How long does a refund take?
A: Refund timing depends on store processing and the payment provider. The assistant should avoid exact promises unless official policy provides them.

Q: Why has a refund not appeared yet?
A: The store may still be inspecting the return, processing the refund, or the payment provider may need more time. The customer should check refund confirmation and bank timelines.

Q: Where is a refund sent?
A: Refunds usually go to the original payment method unless the store offers store credit or another approved method. The demo should not claim it can change refund destination.

Q: Can a refund be sent to a different card?
A: Refunds usually return to the original method for security and compliance. If the card is closed, the customer should contact their bank and official support.

Q: Why is a refund amount lower than expected?
A: Differences may come from shipping fees, discounts, partial returns, restocking fees, taxes, currency conversion, or promotional adjustments.

Q: Can shipping fees be refunded?
A: Shipping fee refunds depend on policy and reason for return. If the store made an error or the item was defective, fees may be handled differently.

Q: What is store credit?
A: Store credit is a balance for future purchases. It may be issued for some returns, promotions, or policy exceptions, and it may have expiration or category limits.

Q: What if a coupon disappears after cancellation?
A: Coupons may not be reusable after cancellation depending on rules. The customer should ask official support whether the coupon can be restored.

Q: Why did payment fail?
A: Payment can fail because of insufficient funds, incorrect card details, bank decline, expired card, network issue, authentication failure, billing address mismatch, or fraud protection.

Q: What should a customer do after a payment failure?
A: The customer should verify card details, billing address, authentication prompts, bank limits, and try another payment method if needed.

Q: Why was money deducted even though the order failed?
A: Some failed payments create temporary authorizations that are released by the payment provider. The customer should check bank timelines and contact support if the hold remains.

Q: Can a customer pay cash on delivery?
A: Cash on delivery depends on region, product category, order value, and store policy. The demo should not claim availability unless the live store supports it.

Q: Why is cash on delivery unavailable?
A: It may be unavailable due to location, high-value items, risk checks, seller restrictions, previous failed deliveries, or category limits.

Q: What payment methods are usually supported?
A: E-commerce stores commonly support cards, wallets, UPI, net banking, gift cards, store credit, and sometimes cash on delivery depending on region.

Q: Is it safe to save a card?
A: Saved cards are usually tokenized by payment providers, but customers should only save payment methods on trusted accounts and devices.

Q: What should a customer do if they see a suspicious charge?
A: The customer should check order history, family purchases, subscriptions, and then contact official support and their bank if the charge remains suspicious.

Q: How should invoice or tax receipt requests be handled?
A: Customers should download invoices from account order history or contact support with the order number. This demo cannot generate official invoices.

Q: Can billing details be changed after purchase?
A: Billing detail changes depend on tax rules and store policy. Some invoices cannot be changed after issue.

Q: Why is a product out of stock?
A: Stock can run out because of high demand, supplier delays, warehouse allocation, discontinued items, or inventory sync delays.

Q: Can a customer reserve an out-of-stock item?
A: Reservation depends on the store. Customers may use back-in-stock alerts or wishlist features where available.

Q: How do back-in-stock alerts work?
A: Customers sign up for a notification, and the store sends an alert when inventory returns. An alert does not always reserve stock.

Q: Why does product price change?
A: Prices can change because of promotions, demand, seller updates, currency, stock levels, or campaign schedules.

Q: Can a customer get a price match after purchase?
A: Price matching depends on policy. The assistant should direct customers to official support and avoid promising adjustments.

Q: How can a customer compare similar products?
A: The customer can use product search, recommendations, filters, descriptions, reviews, specifications, and price information.

Q: How does the recommendation screen work?
A: It looks at historical invoices and recommends products often purchased together with the selected product.

Q: Why are recommendations not personalized?
A: This capstone recommendation feature is based on co-purchase history, not live individual browsing or account behavior.

Q: What if product search returns irrelevant items?
A: The user should try more specific words, product category terms, color, material, or item type. The system ranks text similarity, so phrasing matters.

Q: How does catalog search work?
A: Product descriptions are vectorized and compared with the search query using similarity scoring, then ranked by relevance.

Q: What makes a product rank higher in search?
A: Matching product words, closer semantic or text similarity, and unique terms in the description can raise the ranking.

Q: Can the dashboard answer product availability?
A: It can analyze historical product records but does not connect to live inventory. Availability requires a live inventory integration.

Q: Can the dashboard recommend alternatives for out-of-stock items?
A: It can suggest historically related or text-similar products, but true substitutions require live inventory and product catalog metadata.

Q: What customer data powers churn prediction?
A: Churn prediction uses recency, frequency, monetary value, average order value, total quantity, and product diversity from historical transactions.

Q: What does high churn risk mean?
A: High churn risk means the model estimates the customer may stop buying based on historical behavior. It is a signal for retention action, not a final business decision.

Q: What does medium churn risk mean?
A: Medium churn risk means the customer shows some warning signs and may need monitoring or a targeted retention offer.

Q: What does low churn risk mean?
A: Low churn risk means the customer looks comparatively active or healthy based on the model features.

Q: Why might a recent customer still have churn risk?
A: Risk can remain if frequency is low, monetary value is low, product diversity is limited, or the model learned similar patterns from past customers.

Q: Why might a high-spending customer have churn risk?
A: A high-spending customer can still be risky if they have not purchased recently or if their activity dropped compared with earlier behavior.

Q: Is churn prediction final business advice?
A: No. It is a case-study model that should be validated with business context, test campaigns, and fresh data before real decisions.

Q: How should the business act on churn predictions?
A: Use churn predictions to prioritize retention campaigns, reminders, win-back offers, service recovery, loyalty benefits, and customer research.

Q: What are good retention actions?
A: Good actions include personalized offers, reminders, loyalty points, improved support, product recommendations, subscription prompts, and feedback requests.

Q: What should not be done with churn predictions?
A: Do not deny service, make unfair decisions, or rely on the model without monitoring, fairness checks, and business validation.

Q: How is sales forecasting created?
A: The dashboard builds a time series from historical revenue and forecasts future periods using statistical forecasting methods.

Q: What does forecast MAE mean?
A: Mean absolute error measures the average absolute difference between predicted and actual sales during validation.

Q: What does forecast RMSE mean?
A: Root mean squared error measures forecast error with stronger penalty for large misses.

Q: Why can forecasts be wrong?
A: Forecasts can miss sudden promotions, seasonality shifts, stockouts, holidays, economic changes, or data quality problems.

Q: How should forecasts be used?
A: Forecasts should support planning for inventory, staffing, cash flow, and campaign timing, but they should be reviewed with business context.

Q: What does monitoring show?
A: Monitoring shows data quality, model comparison, forecast metrics, missing customer IDs, returns, cancellations, and evidence for demo readiness.

Q: Why are customer IDs missing?
A: Missing customer IDs may come from guest checkout, incomplete data capture, system migration, privacy handling, or data extraction issues.

Q: Why do returns appear as negative quantities?
A: In many retail datasets, returns or cancellations are represented by negative quantities or invoice markers.

Q: How should cancellation rows be treated?
A: They should be tracked separately because they affect sales, customer behavior, return rate, and product performance.

Q: What is total sales in the dashboard?
A: Total sales is calculated from positive sales rows in the processed transaction data.

Q: What are top products?
A: Top products are ranked by historical sales value or quantity in the processed dataset.

Q: What are top countries?
A: Top countries are markets with the highest historical sales in the transaction data.

Q: What does data quality summary mean?
A: It provides basic counts that help confirm whether the dataset is usable and whether missingness or returns need attention.

Q: What is the AI Studio?
A: AI Studio is the command center that sends structured dashboard metrics to selected MaaS or OpenAI-compatible models to produce executive summaries and strategy recommendations.

Q: What does multi-model comparison do?
A: It asks multiple selected models the same business question so users can compare strategy quality, tone, coverage, and errors.

Q: Why might AI Studio use offline mode?
A: Offline mode is used when no MaaS or OpenAI API key is configured, when the SDK cannot reach the endpoint, or when the model call fails.

Q: Does offline mode still use business data?
A: Yes. Offline mode produces a deterministic brief from dashboard metrics, but it does not call a live language model.

Q: How are MaaS models configured?
A: Set environment variables such as MAAS_BASE_URL, MAAS_API_KEY, MAAS_CHAT_MODEL, MAAS_EMBEDDING_MODEL, and optionally MAAS_VERIFY_SSL before starting Streamlit.

Q: Should API keys be pasted into source code?
A: No. API keys should be stored in environment variables or a secure secret manager. The project includes .env.example for safe configuration.

Q: Why should SSL verification not be disabled casually?
A: Disabling SSL verification weakens connection security. It should only be used in a controlled lab proxy environment if required.

Q: What does retrieval mode auto mean?
A: Auto tries semantic embedding retrieval first and falls back to TF-IDF retrieval if live embeddings are not available.

Q: What does retrieval mode embedding mean?
A: Embedding mode attempts to use the configured embedding model for semantic retrieval. If credentials or endpoint fail, retrieval may return an error mode.

Q: What does retrieval mode TF-IDF mean?
A: TF-IDF mode performs local keyword-weighted retrieval without an API call. It is reliable offline but less semantic than embeddings.

Q: What is top-k retrieval?
A: Top-k retrieval means the system returns the highest scoring knowledge chunks, where k is the number selected in the dashboard.

Q: Why does the dashboard show similarity score?
A: Similarity score helps explain how closely the retrieved source matched the user question. Low scores indicate weaker grounding.

Q: Why does the assistant refuse some answers?
A: It refuses when retrieved context is too weak, preventing unsupported or hallucinated answers.

Q: What is grounded generation?
A: Grounded generation means the answer is produced from retrieved knowledge sources rather than from the model's general memory alone.

Q: What should the assistant do if knowledge is missing?
A: It should say the available context is not enough and advise contacting official support or adding the missing policy to the knowledge base.

Q: How can the knowledge base be improved further?
A: Add official policy documents, return rules, shipping SLA tables, product metadata, warranty terms, escalation scripts, and real support examples.

Q: What is a good support answer style?
A: A good answer is concise, empathetic, specific about next steps, transparent about demo limitations, and grounded in retrieved policy.

Q: How should angry customers be handled?
A: Acknowledge the frustration, ask for order details, avoid blame, explain the next support step, and escalate to official support when needed.

Q: How should urgent delivery requests be handled?
A: Explain that expedited changes require official support or carrier options and that the demo cannot modify shipments.

Q: How should warranty questions be handled?
A: Warranty coverage depends on product category, seller, manufacturer, date of purchase, and condition. The customer should check official warranty policy.

Q: What should a customer do if a product stopped working?
A: They should check warranty eligibility, troubleshooting steps, order proof, and contact official support or manufacturer support.

Q: What should a customer do if accessories are missing?
A: They should check packaging, product listing contents, and contact support with photos and order details.

Q: What should a customer do if size or color is wrong?
A: They should compare the order confirmation with the received item, keep packaging, take photos, and contact support for return or exchange options.

Q: Can a customer edit account email?
A: Email changes are usually handled through account settings or official support. Verification may be required.

Q: Can a customer reset password?
A: The customer should use the official password reset flow and check email or SMS verification. This demo cannot reset passwords.

Q: What if a customer cannot log in?
A: They should verify credentials, reset password, check account email, clear browser issues, and contact support if the account is locked.

Q: Can a customer delete their account?
A: Account deletion depends on privacy policy and legal retention rules. Customers should use official account settings or privacy support.

Q: Can a customer request their data?
A: Data access requests depend on privacy laws and policy. The customer should contact the official privacy or support channel.

Q: How should privacy-sensitive questions be answered?
A: Avoid exposing personal data. Direct the customer to secure official channels and explain that verification is required.

Q: What if a customer asks for another person's order details?
A: The assistant should not provide another person's details and should explain that account verification is required.

Q: Can the project send emails or messages to customers?
A: No. The capstone dashboard does not send live communications. It can suggest campaign ideas or support wording.

Q: Can the project place orders?
A: No. It analyzes historical data and generates support guidance but does not create live orders.

Q: Can the project update warehouse inventory?
A: No. It does not connect to warehouse or inventory systems. Live updates would require a secure integration.

Q: Can the project update payment status?
A: No. Payment changes require a payment gateway or official support system integration.

Q: What integrations would make this production ready?
A: Production readiness would need order management, inventory, payment, carrier tracking, CRM, authentication, logging, monitoring, policy sync, and human escalation.
